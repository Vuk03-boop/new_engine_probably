Status: **passed on the RTX 3050 (2026-09-24), with one known failure awaiting the user** (criterion 4, below). **Update 2026-09-25:** the user chose option A (S-020); with the correction baked from the reference, criterion 4 passes at its declared budget (all arms within 1.2%), see the [sky correction record](2026-09-24-phase3c-sky-correction.md). The uncorrected result below is unchanged and kept as that record's control.
Date and baseline: 2026-09-24, after 3B (no Git).
Authorization: S-017 (slices 3A–3G; sky model delegated: "a good sky makes a lot of difference but leaving it up to your discretion").

## Objective

The sky of the ADR-0005 atmosphere at every time of day, in real time: as seen (pixels without a surface) and as light on the street (one visibility ray per pixel), measured against the 3A Monte Carlo sky.

## What was built

- **`light::sky`** (host, f64, the oracle): Hillaire-2020-style tables from `light::Atmosphere`:
  - transmittance 256×64 (Bruneton's mapping, 128 steps);
  - multiple scattering Ψ_ms 32×32 (64 directions, 20 steps; the isotropic approximation of Hillaire 2020);
  - **ground irradiance** 64×1 (Bruneton 2008; not in Hillaire): the skylight on the planet ground, which lights the ground below the horizon and returns light to the sky;
  - sky view 192×108 per sun (32 steps; azimuth relative to the sun, zenith concentrated at the horizon).
  - Built once in 0.26 s (all but the sky view); ground irradiance and multiple scattering are iterated twice.
  - All integrations use the reference's step layout (`atmosphere::step_bound`) and Hillaire's energy-conserving step integral.
- **`gpu::sky`** + `shaders/sky.slang`, `sky_common.slang`: the sun-independent tables are uploaded from the host (f64 values, no f32 cancellation in the horizon mapping); the sky-view table is rebuilt on the GPU in f32 when the sun moves (altitude carried explicitly: r² − R² = h(2R + h)). About 0.6 MB, `GpuTemporal`.
- **`gpu::shade`, 3C term:** the next two numbers of the pixel's stream pick a cosine direction (the reference's continuation ray at the primary vertex); if it escapes, albedo × table sky. Pixels without a surface show the table sky plus the sun disk. `uniform_sky` is the exact control. Flags `SKY`, `UNIFORM_SKY`; binding 6 is the sky-view table.
- **Viewer:** the sky and its light in the light view; the sky-view table is rebuilt only when the hour changes; display exposure from the sun and the sky's hemisphere mean (the ground-irradiance table / π); `--run-day` starts with the day running.

## Failures on the way (diagnosed)

1. **Transmittance table 1.16% > 1%.** The 40 uniform midpoint steps of Hillaire were each ~12 km long on slanted paths, far coarser than the 1.2 km haze layer. Fixed with the reference's step layout, then 128 steps (the table is built once): 0.53%.
2. **Sky below the horizon at half the reference (dawn), blue deficit up to 15% above it.** Discriminating run (`diagnostic_sky_error_by_elevation_and_ground`): with a black ground the high-sky error mostly disappeared, so the missing term was skylight on the ground. Added Bruneton's ground-irradiance table: below the horizon now −0.2…−7.5%, the high sky within a few percent.
3. **Remaining horizon deficit (3–13% with a black ground).** Not view-ray discretisation (32 and 256 steps agree) and not the multiple-scattering table's sampling (4× finer tables trade it for a zenith overshoot of up to 5%). It is the isotropic multiple-scattering approximation of Hillaire 2020 against the reference's anisotropic all-order transport. It passes criterion 3 but causes criterion 4's failure.

## Criterion 4: the decision for the user

The failure is small and understood, but it is outside a budget that was declared first, so it is not loosened here. Options:
- **A: a correction table.** Bake the ratio (Monte Carlo sky ÷ table sky) once on the GPU with the reference, at low resolution (sun elevation × view zenith × azimuth), store it as a versioned data file next to the code, apply it in the lookup. Expected to bring the bias to the Monte Carlo noise; costs a bake tool, a generated data file (regenerated when the atmosphere changes) and a few minutes of GPU once. It adds a data format, so it needs your yes.
- **B: accept the bias** with the budget for sky light on surfaces set to the measured level (about 6% dawn/dusk, 16% twilight), recorded as the model's known error in ADR-0005.

## Observations

- The sky makes the north-facing shop fronts readable at every hour; sky light is noisy at one ray per pixel (visible grain in the sheet), which the temporal work (3D) and the denoiser (3E) address.
- Display exposure adapts fully to twilight, so the twilight view looks bright; `-` darkens it (display only; a partial-adaptation curve can come with 3E if wanted).
